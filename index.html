Sample No. 01: Blog / Article

Why Brands Feel Like People

They come up with stories: stories that touch the audience's heart, stories that make us remember our past, our pain, or our happiest moments. Sometimes, their product reminds us of someone we know. Sometimes, an advertisement reminds us of a moment we thought we had forgotten.

That advertisement, that storytelling, is what makes a brand feel like a person.

We remember certain advertisements because they tell a story we can relate to. An advertisement might somehow tell the story of our childhood, our first love, our one‑sided love, our family, or even a moment we never knew we wanted to remember.

We take those stories to heart. We talk about them with our colleagues and friends. We share them. And every time we see that product, the advertisement crosses our mind, and the story crosses our heart.

Sometimes, we end up buying the product. But perhaps we weren't buying the product in the first place. We were buying the feeling attached to it.

These brands don't simply sell products. They create meaning around them through stories. We buy the story first, and then we end up buying the product.

Think about a perfume that reminds you of someone you once fell in love with. A particular fragrance can suddenly take you back to a person, a place, or a moment you thought you had moved on from.

Think about food that reminds you of home when you're far away from it. The product isn't just food anymore. It becomes comfort. It becomes familiarity. It becomes home.

That is the power of storytelling.

The best brands don't always try to convince their audience to buy something. Instead, they make their audience feel like they are being understood. They make people think: "That's my story." "I've felt that too." "This reminds me of someone."

And once a brand makes someone feel seen, it becomes more than a brand. It becomes familiar. It becomes memorable. It becomes something people can connect with.

Perhaps that's why the brands we remember most aren't always the ones with the loudest advertisements or the biggest discounts. They're the ones that made us feel something.

Because people may forget what a product promised them. But they rarely forget how a story made them feel.
